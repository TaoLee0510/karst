# Why the high-copy cells of SA906a increase, and what each of the two karyotype landscapes predicted

2026-11-24. The analysis uses benchmark round `6ae2a24`, KARST 1.1.3 and ALFA-K `alfakR_dev2@f93b2d2`. The object of study is the serially passaged SA906a breast epithelial cell culture lineage. The fit uses X15 and X25, and X30 is predicted.

**Conclusion.** From X25→X30, high-copy cells increase from 11/212 (5.19%) to 142/690 (20.58%), but the 11 exact karyotypes of X25 together fall to 3/690 (0.43%). Default KARST assigns these out-of-domain karyotypes a value below the mean division rate of the remaining parental cells. ALFA-K extrapolates to them over long distances and assigns them a value above the mean division rate of the remaining parental cells, and therefore predicts the direction of population movement correctly. After the high-copy fraction of the training data was artificially changed from rising to falling, ALFA-K gives identical predicted rates, item by item, for the same 11 karyotypes. **This control tests whether the model makes use of the change in ploidy group of these out-of-domain cells. ALFA-K builds the landscape from the copy number of each chromosome; there is direct code and data evidence for this.**

## 1. What is compared, and the actual change

A karyotype consists of the integer copy numbers of 22 autosomes. Here the cell ploidy index is defined as the arithmetic mean of the 22 numbers, and a mean ≥3 (sum ≥66) is counted in the high-copy group. This operational definition is suitable for checking population change; assigning lineages to whole-genome doubling needs additional genomic evidence. The denominator of a frequency is all included observed cells of that passage. “Original 11 types” requires all 22 positions to be identical. Fig. 2c compares two 22-dimensional displacement vectors that start from the same parent, namely the predicted daughter mean minus the parental mean and the actual daughter mean minus the parental mean.

| Passage | Total cells | High-copy cells | High-copy fraction | Cells matching the original 11 types |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| X10 | 823 | 34 | 4.13% | 3 |
| X15 | 767 | 12 | 1.56% | 0 |
| X25 | 212 | 11 | 5.19% | 11 |
| X30 | 690 | 142 | 20.58% | 3 |
| X40 | 396 | 137 | 34.60% | 0 |
| X50 | 389 | 248 | 63.75% | 0 |
| X57 | 446 | 312 | 69.96% | 1 |

The 11 high-copy cells of X25 each have a different karyotype. The 142 high-copy cells of X30 belong to 88 karyotypes, of which only 2 karyotypes, with 3 cells, match the original 11 types. Cells whose exact labels differ may still share a common ancestor. Assuming that the two samplings are independent, that every cell enters the sample with equal probability and that the true high-copy fraction is constant, the two-sided Fisher exact test of X25 against X30 gives `p=1.61×10⁻⁸`. This result and the later passages support a change in population composition, but on their own they cannot distinguish selection, ploidy state transitions, passage bottlenecks or sampling bias. The original study also identified, in this culture branch, a whole-genome-doubled clone with a focal MDM4 amplification, which made up about 57% by the last sampling; in this analysis that clone was not matched one by one to these 11 labels. ## 2. How the two methods assign values to the same cells

**KARST.** The direct fit domain contains the selected major karyotypes and their one-step neighbours. The 12 high-copy cells of X15 and the 11 of X25 all fall outside the domain. The prediction still keeps the complete X25 parent. For karyotypes that were observed in training but excluded from the direct fit domain, in this case `predict()` uses the mean log division rate of the observed non-major karyotypes in the domain; taking the exponential gives a common geometric-mean division rate. The default fit gives each of the original 11 types a division rate of 0.885/day, while the weighted mean of the other 201 parental cells is 1.005/day. This rule by itself does not prescribe that high-copy karyotypes should grow slowly. For out-of-domain karyotypes never seen in training, the model has separate dosage, curvature and distance extrapolation rules. The two kinds of out-of-domain prediction need to be kept apart. [Direct fit domain and prediction implementation](../../../R/sparse-joint.R) · [Script that reconstructs the assignment](../results_6ae2a24/analysis/fig2c_two_timepoints/oracle_both/benchmark_audit/X25_predictors/trace_rates.R)

**ALFA-K.** This case fixes `minobs=5`. The counts at the two training time points are summed by exact karyotype; 6 karyotypes reach the threshold, and another 242 one-step neighbours provide division-rate estimates. The 248 positions used to build the kriging landscape have total copy numbers in the range 42–46; the original 11 types have 66–108 and are 20–62 single-copy steps away from the nearest fitted position. The complete-parent comparison reuses the rows already present in the archived ALFA-K landscape, and the additional karyotypes are filled in by its kriging model based on bootstrap means. All of the original 11 types use extrapolation. The karyotype list of this experiment is wider than the paper's original exported list. [Implementation of the landscape filling](../R/align.R) · [Counts, distances and assignments per karyotype](../results_6ae2a24/analysis/fig2c_two_timepoints/oracle_both/benchmark_audit/X25_predictors/parent_rate_components.tsv)

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 11 high-copy cells | 0.18544 | −0.00010 | 0.18535 | 0.18535 |
| The other 201 cells | 0.21632 | −0.09158 | 0.12474 | 0.12195 |

Splitting the kriging prediction into a fixed trend and a local residual shows that **the linear trend alone even gives the high-copy group a slightly lower mean; what determines the ordering is a large downward correction for the other parental cells, while the local correction of the distant 11 types is almost zero.** For example, the standard diploid karyotype accounts for 96/212 cells at X25, and its division rate input to the simulator is only 0.07294/day. The last column differs slightly from the sum of the first two because 200 of the other 201 cells use the original archived landscape mean. ## 3. How to test whether ALFA-K learned the rise of the high-copy group from the out-of-domain karyotypes

The proposition to be tested is: keeping the high-frequency karyotypes, the neighbour karyotypes and their counts unchanged, and reversing only the change in high-copy fraction carried by the out-of-domain labels, does ALFA-K's division-rate estimate for the original 11 types change? This is a paired refit intervention. The Fig. 2c angle or the raw group means alone cannot answer this question.

1. **Fix the original input and the query objects.** Read the X15 and X25 count columns in `SA906a_X25_l_2_d1_1_d2_1.Rds`. First save the labels of the 11 high-copy karyotypes that were originally to be queried. Each of these rows has a count of 0 at X15 and 1 at X25; none of these 11 rows is among the 248 fitted positions.
2. **Change only the row names, to reverse the compositional trend.** In the control copy, these 11 labels are replaced by 11 karyotypes that are distinct from each other and appear neither in the original input nor among the fitted positions. Each new karyotype starts from the all-diploid karyotype and gains one copy at each of 6 positions, so its total copy number is 50 and it falls in the low-copy group. An element-by-element check confirms that the complete count matrix with row names removed is identical, and that row order, column totals, the high-frequency set and the one-step neighbour counts are the same. The high-copy fraction of the original data is 1.56%→5.19%; in the control it becomes 1.56%→0%.
3. **Paired refit.** Both inputs use random seed 77, with 500 bootstrap draws each. Both arms fix `minobs=5`, a missegregation rate per copy per division of `5×10⁻⁵`, and initial and final population size parameters of `10⁵` and `10⁷`, and enable the missegregation outflow correction. The high-frequency fitness fit reads the high-frequency rows; the neighbour fit reads the corresponding neighbour rows and uses the total sample depth. The 11 renamed rows remain outside both kinds of positions. The full matrix and the row order are kept, so that the same random numbers correspond to the same count draws.
4. **Compare the intermediate quantities, then the original labels.** First compare the two arms' 500×248 bootstrap division rates; then build a stable kriging landscape from the column means of each arm, and query both at the **original** 11 labels saved in the first step. Querying the new 50-copy labels instead would change the query positions at the same time, and could not isolate the training signal.

| Quantity checked | Original training input | Relabelled training input |
|---|---:|---:|
| X15 high-copy fraction | 1.56% | 1.56% |
| X25 high-copy fraction | 5.19% | 0% |
| Bootstrap draws × fitted positions | 500 × 248 | 500 × 248 |
| Mean predicted division rate of the original 11 types, per day | 0.3199866 | 0.3199866 |

**Result.** The 124,000 fitted rates of the two arms are identical item by item, and the maximum absolute difference between the individual predictions for the original 11 types is also 0. For this window and threshold, the ALFA-K landscape did not use the high-copy identity of these 11 rows to learn the rise in group fraction. The chromosome copy-number composition still determines where a karyotype sits in the landscape; in the real prediction, the 11 high-copy cells of X25 still determine the starting point of the simulation. The control only refits the landscape; it did not initialise the simulation with the relabelled X25, nor did it use X30 for fitting.

**Limits on the absolute division rates.** Both refits completed 500 draws, and optimisation warnings appeared during the run. The two-arm mean of 0.3200/day differs clearly from the 0.1853/day of the original archive; the present control does not separate the sources of this between-run difference. The strongest evidence is that the effect of changing labels under the same random conditions is zero, together with the specific source of the two groups' ordering in the archived landscape. Karyotypes near the boundary, karyotypes that enter the high-frequency or neighbour sets, and other thresholds need separate tests. ## 4. Testing the population direction with a common simulator

Both methods start from the same complete X25 frequencies and run the same version of the individual-based simulator on the same list of 52,080 karyotypes, with random seeds 1–5 and a duration of 25 days. Each of the three landscapes is multiplied by a constant so that the mean division rate of the X25 parent, weighted by initial cell count, is 0.0921034/day for all of them. This value is the common baseline for the comparison. Low-division-rate KARST uses a converged refit that keeps 50 days of history. The angle comes from the population displacement after equally weighted averaging over the five seeds. [Simulation, calibration and scoring script](../results_6ae2a24/analysis/fig2c_two_timepoints/oracle_both/benchmark_audit/X25_predictors/common_parent_mean/run.R) · [Calibration table](../results_6ae2a24/analysis/fig2c_two_timepoints/oracle_both/benchmark_audit/X25_predictors/common_parent_mean/calibration.tsv)

| Observed or predicted | Mean division rate, high-copy group / remaining parental cells, per day | X30 high-copy fraction | X30 original 11 types among all cells | Chromosomes whose mean rises | Fig. 2c angle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Actual observation | no per-karyotype truth | 20.58% | 0.43% | 22/22 | — |
| ALFA-K | 0.1363 / 0.0897 | 12.20% | 11.84% | 21/22 | 34.04° |
| Default KARST | 0.0816 / 0.0927 | 3.95% | 3.92% | 0/22 | 162.38° |
| Low-division-rate KARST | 0.0852 / 0.0925 | 3.47% | 3.44% | 0/22 | 128.11° |

**Locating the direction error.** Keeping the within-group frequencies of the X30 predicted by default KARST and changing only the total fraction of the high-copy group to the true 20.58%, the angle drops from 162.38° to 10.39°; for low-division-rate KARST it drops from 128.11° to 13.25°. Even freezing the within-group composition of X25 and changing only the shares of the two groups to the values observed at X30 gives a direction only 10.26° away from the actual one. These are post hoc attributions that use the known daughter. In ALFA-K's real prediction, 97.05% of the predicted high-copy cells belong to the original 11 types, whereas in reality only 2.11% do. Predicting the population direction correctly and being able to identify the exact karyotypes that expand are therefore two independent evaluations. ## 5. Relation to the other benchmark results

- **Fig. 2c is sensitive to the choice of window and threshold.** In the 44 paired comparisons with a fixed ALFA-K training window, the complete parent and a common simulator, native-scale KARST wins 23 pairs on the angle and ALFA-K wins 21. Another retrospective experiment, on 155 “daughter and training window” combinations where both sides have results, first let each method pick its own best window using the true daughter; if the ALFA-K threshold is chosen by training cross-validation, the wins of KARST and ALFA-K are 24 to 20; if the true daughter is also allowed to pick ALFA-K's threshold, they become 17 to 27. The denominator is 44 daughters in both cases. These results show that the selection rule needs to be reported, and also that the single-case conclusion for SA906a cannot be generalised to the whole cohort. [Fixed-window experiment](../results_6ae2a24/analysis/fig2c_two_timepoints/shared_full_parent/README.md) · [Threshold-selection control](../results_6ae2a24/analysis/fig2c_two_timepoints/oracle_both/benchmark_audit/README.md)
- **Fig. 2d measures a different quantity.** The original figure asks whether the distances of change from parent to daughter are ranked correctly. Comparing the two daughter predictions directly with the true daughter instead, KARST has the smaller transport distance in 37 of 45 pairs and the higher exact-karyotype frequency overlap in 35. Of the 18 pairs in the SA609 family where it beats ALFA-K on both, 13 also beat simply keeping the parental composition on both. What is compared here is another set of predictions that can be paired in the original Fig. 2d. [Three paired daughter tests of Fig. 2d](fig2d_salehi_paired_daughters_6ae2a24.md)
- **A common missegregation rate did not make the landscapes coincide.** On the same set of 252 Salehi two-point windows that have landscapes from both sides at all seven missegregation-rate levels, with `minobs=5` fixed and comparison by complete karyotype: at the default missegregation rate, the mean absolute difference over the high-frequency karyotypes within a window, with the median then taken over windows, is 0.250/day, and the median rank correlation is 0.688; for the non-high-frequency one-step neighbours the values are 0.262/day and 0.643. These measure agreement between the methods. Real data have no per-karyotype true division rates. [Paired results at the seven missegregation-rate levels](../results_6ae2a24/analysis/fig2c_two_timepoints/oracle_both/benchmark_audit/X25_predictors/landscape_sampling_audit/grid_summary_common_windows.tsv)
- **This case is not caused by the handling of negative division rates.** The simulation code of the ALFA-K paper era deletes clones with non-positive division rates within one step; the version that Ver10 uses for both landscapes and KARST's own benchmark both keep non-positive values and let them not divide; for positive values, the latter two draw the number of divisions from a capped Poisson and a binomial, respectively. Under the common scale of this case, all input rates of the three landscapes are positive. In the paired simulations of KARST's own benchmark, the 3,200 core datasets with wavelength ≥0.4 have exactly identical counts after the deletion rule is switched. At the rugged wavelength 0.2, a few trajectories separate clearly; how many wins switching the whole generator would change has not yet been isolated and quantified. [Paired experiment on non-positive division rates](../../../doc/notes/nonpositive_fitness.md)
- **Scope of the conclusions.** This document explains Fig. 2c for a specific window and for the complete-parent version; the threshold and window come from earlier retrospective analyses, which included picking the best by the actual daughter. It answers how the two models assign values, and why a direction gap appears on this pair. Whether ploidy trends can improve forward prediction should be judged on independent lineages or on strict temporal hold-out results. [Comparison of windows, thresholds and cohort](../results_6ae2a24/analysis/fig2c_two_timepoints/oracle_both/benchmark_audit/README.md)
