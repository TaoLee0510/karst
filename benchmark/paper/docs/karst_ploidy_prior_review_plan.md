# Using the ploidy distribution for KARST's inference on rare karyotypes: literature basis and execution plan

2026-11-24. This document proposes a model extension that is still to be validated. The case diagnosis is complete; the model, simulations and forward validation below have not yet been carried out. [SA906a case and relabelling experiment](sa906a_alfak_karst_high_copy_6ae2a24.md)

## 1. The scientific question suggested to be answered first

**Given complete karyotype counts at two passages, can the recent change in the ploidy distribution improve the forecast of the next passage?** The suggestion is to keep KARST's 22-dimensional karyotypes, missegregation transitions and per-karyotype division rates, and to add a group effect that can shrink towards zero for karyotypes that lack direct fit evidence. First compare three models: the current KARST; KARST that lets all out-of-domain karyotypes share a rate offset; and KARST that additionally lets high-copy out-of-domain karyotypes have an extra offset. Only if the third model beats the second in independent evaluation is there evidence to attribute the gain to ploidy information.

**Why is the second model needed?** Joining the complete X15 and X25 counts of SA906a row by row with the original KARST fit domain gives the table below. High copy is defined as a mean copy number ≥3 over the 22 autosomes; in this window there are no in-domain high-copy cells.

| Training time point | In-domain low copy | Out-of-domain low copy | Out-of-domain high copy | High-copy share of all cells | Out-of-domain share of all cells | High-copy share of out-of-domain cells |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| X15 | 751 | 4 | 12 | 12/767 = 1.56% | 16/767 = 2.09% | 12/16 = 75.00% |
| X25 | 196 | 5 | 11 | 11/212 = 5.19% | 16/212 = 7.55% | 11/16 = 68.75% |

The relative share of the whole out-of-domain group rises, the conditional share of high copy within the two groups falls slightly, and at each time point there are only 16 out-of-domain cells. Looking only at the overall high-copy frequency would mix the two phenomena. Computed from the training data only, the log odds of the overall high-copy share increase on average by 0.0247/day over 50 days. This number can serve as a reference for a group-trend forecast; when there is cross-group missegregation, cell death or a sampling difference, it cannot be added directly to the cell division rate. ## 2. Which modelling choices the literature supports

This is a targeted literature survey, as of 2026-11-24, of original experimental and methods papers on single-cell karyotypes, ploidy, whole-genome doubling, chromosome missegregation, clonal selection and compositional count models. The table below separates experimental observations, assumption-dependent model results, and design choices that can be transferred to KARST.

| Original study | Conclusion directly supported by the study | Implication for KARST |
|---|---|---|
| [Salehi et al., 2021, longitudinal single-cell clonal fitness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8396073/) | The authors estimated relative selection from longitudinal clone frequencies and a population-genetics model; in the culture branch that corresponds to this case, the expanding clone has both whole-genome doubling and a focal MDM4 amplification. | Ploidy can mark an expanding lineage. From a ploidy time series alone it is hard to separate ploidy itself, linked drivers and other clonal features. |
| [Dewhurst et al., 2014, diploid and tetraploid cells of the same genetic background](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4293454/) | Surviving tetraploid colon cancer cells tolerate chromosome abnormalities caused by missegregation better; instability computed per chromosome did not rise in step. | Cells of the same ploidy class can share statistical information. Per-division errors, daughter survival and cell division rate should be handled separately. |
| [Watson, Lee et al., 2024, chromosome screens in breast and kidney epithelial cells](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-024-01665-2) | In-vitro proliferation screens yielded tissue-specific karyotypes; same-background clone experiments support a proliferative advantage for certain specific copy changes. | A fixed positive “high-ploidy bonus” should not be imposed on all sources. Allow differences between sources and negative effects. |
| [Beck, Li, Andor, original ALFA-K paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12848095/) | The authors compared the fitness effects of single-chromosome gains and losses on different ploidy backgrounds. Their grouping for the whole-genome-doubling analysis uses the mode of the chromosome copy numbers. | Ploidy background can affect the local karyotype landscape. This analysis cannot directly validate the long-distance extrapolation of SA906a; the grouping in the original paper also differs from the mean-copy-number threshold of this document. |
| [Lynch et al., 2022, Bayesian inference of karyotype diversity and chromosomal instability](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9054132/) | In an individual-based model the authors compared the cost of deviating from balanced euploidy, chromosome driver-gene density and mixed selection models, showing that karyotype composition is shaped by both the missegregation rate and selection. | Suitable for listing “chromosome imbalance on the same ploidy background” as a later candidate, and for testing the confounding of selection and state transitions with simulations of known missegregation rate. |
| [Dinh et al., 2025, CINner model](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012902) | The model represents whole-genome-doubling events, missegregation, survival and selection separately; different mechanisms can change the observed ploidy and copy-number distributions. | A negative control of “inflow into the high-copy group only, no high-copy fitness advantage” should be arranged, to check whether the fit wrongly estimates a positive division-rate effect. |
| [Büttner et al., 2021, hierarchical model of single-cell compositional counts](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-27150-6) | The authors jointly modelled cell-type counts with a hierarchical Dirichlet–multinomial distribution, handling relative abundance and between-sample variation. | The ideas of compositional counts and shrinkage can be borrowed. The model itself has no karyotype transitions or birth process, and its abundance coefficients cannot directly replace KARST's division rates. |

**The literature supports introducing shared information, but cannot provide KARST with a universal daily reward coefficient.** The prior on the log division-rate offset should be centred at zero and allow both directions; the prior scale can come from independent training sources or from simulations with known truth. The two ploidy counts of the current lineage itself are observed data and should update the group effect through the likelihood. Writing the slope computed from these two time points into the prior and also using it as the fit target would make the same data appear twice.

## 3. Where the current code can be extended

- `sparse_joint_domain()` in `R/sparse-joint.R` already gives domain membership, the excluded karyotypes and the in-domain counts; the original full counts are stored in the fit object. Before sparse modelling, the raw counts of the out-of-domain high and low groups can be computed per time point, with a check that, together with the in-domain cell numbers, they sum to the total depth. [Fit domain and count entry point](../../../R/sparse-joint.R)
- The current trend contains the linear dose of each chromosome, quadratic terms and the distance from the major karyotype. Simply adding “mean copy number” as another linear covariate column would be linearly dependent on the existing dose columns; moreover, when predicting out-of-domain karyotypes already observed in training, `predict()` overrides the ordinary trend value. Adding one column to the design matrix cannot fix this case. Start with a two-group contrast; to study continuous ploidy, explicitly decompose the total dose and zero-sum chromosome contrasts, or use a constrained nonlinear term. [Trend design and out-of-domain fallback](../../../R/sparse-joint.R)
- The two-time-point objective already places a normal trend prior on the log division rate and fits in-domain karyotypes with Poisson counts. The new group parameters must be used consistently in training-period propagation, out-of-domain prediction, full parent export and prediction uncertainty; old fit objects read in by different software versions should keep following the original rules. [Two-point objective](../../../src/sparse_joint_model.h) · [Simulation and calibration example](../results_6ae2a24/analysis/fig2c_two_timepoints/oracle_both/benchmark_audit/X25_predictors/common_parent_mean/run.R)

**Difference from earlier attempts.** The repository's R10 attempt added transitions from the major karyotype to doubled karyotypes; in the original simulation almost all cells were already doubled at the first sampling point, so expansion could not be tested. The later C2, with another set of settings, had too small a high-ploidy population. D3 improved part of the SA906a scores but did not pass the overall test. This plan first tests whether the observed out-of-domain populations can share rate information, keeping the existing missegregation kernel; the existing whole-genome-doubling simulations can be used for stress tests. ## 4. The minimal model suggested for testing

Let $D$ be the direct fit domain determined by a training window, and let $H(k)$ indicate whether the mean copy number over the 22 autosomes of karyotype $k$ is ≥3. Write $b^0_k$ for KARST's base division rate of this karyotype, in units per day. Out-of-domain karyotypes observed in training keep the common base level; out-of-domain karyotypes not seen in training use their original trend base value. The first version only adds a group offset to the out-of-domain part:

$$
\log b_{k,s}=\log b^0_{k,s}
+\mathbf{1}_{k\notin D_s}\bigl(u_s+\delta_s H(k)\bigr).
$$

$s$ denotes the fit window. $u_s$ is the offset shared by all out-of-domain karyotypes, and $\delta_s$ is the extra offset of out-of-domain high copy relative to out-of-domain low copy. Both are **the log of a division-rate multiplier**; the original rate multiplied by $\exp(u_s+\delta_s)$ is still positive. Give $u_s$ and $\delta_s$ separate zero-centred normal priors. In development, it is suggested to compare frozen prior widths, e.g. with the 95% prior multiplier roughly in $[1/1.5,1.5]$ or $[1/2,2]$, plus an ablation group with width zero. The values are candidate design parameters, not biological constants measured in the literature. The width may only be chosen with development simulations or with sources outside the outer held-out set; when a single window has only two count time points, the width is fixed. The group offset obtained from one posterior draw is used jointly by all karyotypes of the group, to avoid treating the evidence of one group as many independent precise estimates.

**How each cell enters the estimate.** At each time point only one mutually exclusive observation is recorded: in-domain karyotypes are counted individually, and out-of-domain cells go into two baskets, “out-of-domain low” and “out-of-domain high”. At the same sampling depth, the model predicts an intensity $\lambda_{k,t}$ for each in-domain state and an intensity $\Lambda_{g,t}=\sum_{k\in g}\lambda_{k,t}$ for each of the two outside groups. The Poisson model can be kept, with likelihood terms added for these two out-of-domain total counts; alternatively, given the total depth, a multinomial likelihood over these mutually exclusive categories can be used. The in-domain counts already used are not put again into an extra “whole-sample high-copy fraction” likelihood term. When both groups have counts, the contrast with the in-domain population is still kept; fitting a proportion only within the 16 out-of-domain cells would discard the strongest population-change signal in this case.

**How to obtain the group intensities.** The prototype first runs a deterministic propagation of expected values from the complete frequencies at the training start: on a controlled list covering the observed karyotypes and their neighbours, it computes inflow, outflow and daughter survival between karyotypes under the current missegregation kernel, then sums by group. High-copy out-of-domain descendants follow the same group rule as in training. Track the probability mass not covered by the list boundary, and after extending the list by one layer check the group frequencies and division-rate offsets; validate the expected-value propagation with the individual-based simulator under the same settings. In fitting, the counts at the training end point are used only as end-point observations, and their karyotype frequencies must not be used to fill in the start point; for zero counts at the start, allow a small initial mass under finite detection depth, and check how this assumption and state inflow affect the conclusions. If the group effect is almost indistinguishable from inflow, report the wide interval and the stability of its forecast. When a group lacks observations it is shrunk by the prior; when there is a common rise but high and low cannot be told apart, allow it to be explained mainly by $u_s$.

## 5. Execution order, controls and decisions

| Stage | Specific work and outputs | Decision basis |
|---|---|---|
| I. Freeze data and controls | Use the 44 fully observed daughters, 35 parents and 5 source families as the historical control panel. Freeze the training windows, the actual passage intervals, the absolute copy-number convention, the baseline models, the grouping threshold, the group counts, the domain coverage and the seeds; SA906a X30 is used only for the known-case diagnosis. Output the mutually exclusive count table per window and the split by source. | The group totals reproduce the total depth column by column; the per-karyotype data can rebuild the existing shares and angles. Mean ≥3 is the main definition; mode ≥3 only as a pre-declared sensitivity check. |
| II. Implement a research prototype that can be switched off | First restrict to two time points. In separate research code, implement the extra group counts, between-group propagation and the shrunk $u,\delta$. Compare original KARST, the same new observation layer with $u=\delta=0$ locked, only $u$ released, and both $u,\delta$ released. Record fit status, parameter correlations, differences between posterior and prior, boundary mass and run time. | With the extension switched off, the original forecast is replayed; comparing locked and released parameters within the same new observation layer isolates the ploidy information. Check rises and falls, zero counts, everything in domain, first appearance at the end point, swapped labels, and permutation of karyotypes within a group. |
| III. Validate first in simulations with known truth | Reuse the existing missegregation simulations and the whole-genome-doubling generator. Include a common out-of-domain advantage without a ploidy effect, positive and negative high-ploidy effects, neutral drift, state transitions or doubling inflow only, a single clone dominating within a same-ploidy group, low sequencing depth and passage bottlenecks. Keep the seeds and landscapes for debugging separate from those for the final evaluation. Output recovery of the true division rates, group-effect coverage and forecast error. | First check whether both groups have discernible mass at sampling. Scenarios are checked only by their true settings and sampling coverage; trajectories are not screened by method score. If zero-effect or pure-inflow scenarios frequently produce credible positive effects, the model output cannot be interpreted as ploidy selection. |
| IV. Forecast real daughters over time | After freezing the parameters, fit each historical pair of training time points, initialise with the complete parent, and forecast the next daughter with the same karyotype list, the same simulator, the same set of seeds and a consistent rate scale. Add: parent composition held unchanged, extrapolation only by the slope of the group log odds over the two training points, the current KARST, and ALFA-K with the original cross-validation rule. The reference time scale is used for all models; each model's native scale is reported separately. | Windows with a history tune parameters only with earlier passages. Across sources, whole families are held out, so that shared ancestors, parents or daughters do not enter both training and evaluation. The 44 pairs have already been explored and should be marked retrospective; generalisation is also confirmed with simulations that were frozen and not used in model selection, or with independent absolute copy-number time series. |
| V. Decide whether it is worth merging into the main line | List all forecasts and failures per daughter; results that share a parent are first summarised within the parent, then reported separately for SA609, SA906, the remaining families, and with sources weighted equally. First report the comparisons where the real passage intervals can be aligned, and list separately the differences from the historical fixed forecast horizon. | The main comparison is “common out-of-domain offset + ploidy offset” vs “common out-of-domain offset only”; also compare with the original KARST. When the extra ploidy effect cannot be estimated, the simpler model can be kept as a candidate. |

**Primary end point.** Pre-define the posterior predictive log score of the daughter's high-copy count, with each daughter as the unit, aggregated with sources weighted equally; higher scores are better. The forecast distribution must include the uncertainty of the parameters, the simulation and finite cell sampling; shallow samples in which no high-copy cell was observed also take part in scoring. Also report the absolute error of the high-copy frequency and the direction of the group change, and check the coverage of the forecast intervals. **Karyotype-distribution safeguard metrics** use the Wasserstein distance of the complete 22-dimensional distribution and the exact karyotype frequency overlap; the Fig. 2c angle is a secondary, directional metric. Angles that are undefined for zero displacement are listed separately. Also report how much the observed high-copy labels and the new labels each account for, to guard against obtaining a nice angle from a wrong within-group composition.

**Decision suggested to be fixed before opening the confirmation set.** Merging into the default model is discussed only if, in held-out forecasts, the ploidy term improves the primary end point relative to “out-of-domain offset only”, the improvement replicates outside SA906, and the karyotype-distribution safeguard metrics do not exceed a pre-agreed substantive degradation. The safeguard tolerance is set from the repeated-seed fluctuation in development and the observation resampling error, then frozen. When there are few sources, show the paired difference for each source and the leave-one-source-out results; even if one family contributes many time windows, it cannot stand in for many independent experiments. Improvements obtained before confirmation are only candidate results.

**The first executable deliverable** is the complete “passage × fit domain × ploidy group” count table, plus a tally of three kinds of signal: a common out-of-domain increase, an out-of-domain increase of high ploidy relative to low ploidy, and the two in opposite directions. Each row of the table keeps the source, the parent and daughter IDs, the two training passages, the interval in days, the numerators of the four mutually exclusive categories and the total depth. First reproduce SA906a's 751/4/12 and 196/5/11 on the same table, then list per window the two kinds of composition change and their sampling intervals. This step only needs to read the existing inputs and fit objects to judge how many windows contain information that separates $u$ from $\delta$.

**Suggested second scope decision.** After the tally is complete and the evaluation rules are frozen, decide whether to run the simulations and implement the research prototype. The initial two-group model uses only one split of the ploidy distribution. Only if the two-group contrast shows a stable, independent gain should multi-group distributions covering low, middle and high ploidy be compared, with complexity limited by a zero-centred shrinkage prior on the differences between adjacent group effects; if only the common out-of-domain offset is supported, improving the out-of-domain fallback takes priority. Both outcomes can answer the scientific question exposed by SA906a.
