# alfak2 versus alfakR on the ALFA-K paper tests: comparison report

> Terms (changed 2026-11-15): the input columns are called "time points", not "samples". The text of this document has been changed; the figures in `results/figures/` still carry the original
> labels ("2 samples", "all samples"), because this round lacks the input coverage tables that the later analysis code needs (`tables/abm_inputs.Rds` etc.),
> so the current code cannot redraw them without re-summarising. "samples" in the figures means time points.

> The alfak2 ff13cf4 round (history chosen by marginal likelihood, inputs that cannot be analysed excluded from the Salehi data) is reported separately in
> `docs/report_ff13cf4.md`, with results in `results_ff13cf4/`. This document covers the 8327282 round, with results in `results/`.

Date: 2026-11-13. alfak2 version 8327282 (`hpc/alfak2.lock`), alfakR version alfakR_dev2 f93b2d2. Figures and tables are generated by
`scripts/analyse_abm.R` and `scripts/analyse_salehi.R`; tables are in `BENCH_RESULTS/analysis/{abm,salehi}/`,
figures in `BENCH_RESULTS/figures/` (written as `figures/…` in the text).

## 1. Conclusions

1. **With two-time-point input, alfak2 beats alfakR on most tests, including alfakR using all time points.** On the paper's ABM data,
   when the two time points are 3 or 7 passages apart, in paired comparisons on the same input, alfak2's landscape accuracy is higher on 88–100% of inputs (λ ≥ 0.4),
   and the fraction of forecasts beating the "no evolution" baseline at passage 1 is about 0.91, versus about 0.61 for alfakR with two time points and about 0.74 for alfakR with all time points.
2. **When the two time points are only 1 passage apart (about 6–10 days), alfak2's advantage disappears and long-range forecasts get worse.** At λ = 0.4 the landscape accuracy is
   level with alfakR; from passage 3 onward the forecasts fall below the baseline (Wasserstein win fraction at passage 10 is 0.26, alfakR 0.32),
   and at a high missegregation rate (8e-4) the median Pearson r is only 0.04 (alfakR 0.33). This is the "short-interval" weakness that has already been handed off
   (`alfak2/HANDOFF_CLAUDE_2026-11-12.md`); 8327282, tested here, does not include the later history changes.
3. **On real data (Salehi), alfak2's forecasts win more often in pairs, regardless of interval.** Same trajectory, same minobs:
   the fraction beating the parent baseline at the evaluation passage is 0.34–0.48 for alfak2 and 0.21–0.23 for alfakR with two time points (significant for all four distances);
   alfak2 is higher at gaps from 1 to 6 passages. But both methods' win fractions are below 0.5, consistent with the paper: on these data, forecasts
   are mostly worse than "the next passage is the same as the parent".
4. **alfak2's fits do not always complete on real data.** 24 of the 277 Salehi trajectories (all minobs failed)
   raise the error "A timepoint has no cell inside the model domain", concentrated in SA609-derived lineages such as SA000, SA001, SA609H;
   alfakR can fit these trajectories.
5. **CV scores cannot be compared across methods.** alfak2's CV scores are generally higher (Salehi median 0.86 vs alfakR 0.03–0.47),
   but this is caused by the two methods having different cross-validation targets (see Section 6), and does not mean the accuracy differs this much. When filtering by CV score,
   the two methods are tested on different inputs, so the results reported under the paper's rule in this document should be read together with the paired results.
6. **Side finding: alfakR dev2 fit with all time points and minobs 5 is clearly worse than the paper's version.** With 8 time points and minobs 5,
   the median Pearson r at λ = 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.6 is 0.05 / 0.27 / −0.00 respectively, versus 0.39 / 0.72 / 0.79 for the paper's version;
   at minobs 10 and 20 the two agree. PANcanKFLs uses exactly this version and this set of minobs.

## 2. Setup

| Fit | Input |
|---|---|
| alfak2 2 time points | the first and last time points of the window |
| alfakR 2 time points | the same two time points |
| alfakR all time points | all time points of the window (the paper's approach); for the 2-passage window this is the same fit as the row above |
| paper alfakR | the paper's own fits, as a reference line only |

* ABM: 400 simulations (λ = 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.6, 100 each); the windows are the last 2, 4, 8 passages before day 120,
  with first and last 1, 3, 7 passages apart; minobs 5, 10, 20. Missegregation-rate experiment: 5 missegregation rates × 25 simulations, minobs 10.
* Salehi: 277 trajectories, each of 2 to 10 consecutive passages, with first and last 1 to 9 passages apart; minobs 5, 10, 20.
* Number of fits: ABM 9600 (alfak2 3600, alfakR 6000), missegregation rate 1000, Salehi 2277.

**Reproduction check.** Rescoring the paper's own fits with the scoring code here, the scores of 3261 ABM fits and 831 Salehi fits are exactly the same as the paper's
`ABM_summaries.Rds` and `alfak_outputs_proc`; Fig. 2b, 2d, 2f
(per lineage), 2h and Fig. 3e–f recomputed on the paper's fits by the ported analysis scripts agree with the paper's source data (Fig. 3f: f most significant 7/35, d1 most significant 23/35).

**Two ways of reporting.** "Paper rule": failed fits are dropped; where the paper makes a selection, minobs is selected by each fit's own CV score
(for Salehi the trajectory length as well), so different methods are tested on different inputs. "Paired": only the same inputs for which both methods have results,
with no selection.

**Failure rates.** ABM: alfak2 0/3600; alfakR two time points 79/3600 (2.2%), all time points 61/3600 (1.7%),
mostly with the error "Regression matrix for fixed part of model is colinear". Salehi: see Section 4.1. All
1000 fits of the missegregation-rate experiment completed; 3 of them, alfakR all-time-point fits (missegregation rate 4e-4), first exceeded memory and completed after rerunning with 64 GB and 256 GB.

## 5. Stability: change with the interval between the two time points

* **ABM** (`figures/stability_by_window.png`): both methods' accuracy rises as the interval increases (1 → 3 → 7 passages).
  alfak2 gains most from 1 to 3 passages (at λ = 0.8 the median R² goes from 0.08 to 0.65); alfakR with two time points gains less
  (0.04 to 0.33). Between 3 and 7 passages both are essentially stable.
* **Salehi**: alfak2 is above alfakR with two time points at 1 to 6 passages apart and does not get worse with the interval; the win fraction of alfakR with two time points
  drops to 0.16 or below at 4 passages and more.
* Short intervals are alfak2's weak point: at 1 passage apart, both the ABM long-range forecasts and the landscapes at high missegregation rate are worse than alfakR's.

## 6. Why CV scores cannot be compared across methods

In both packages the CV score is R2R = 1 − Σ(p′ − o′)² / Σo′² (p, o centred), which equals 2rs − s²,
where r is the correlation and s is the ratio of the standard deviations of prediction and target; it has no lower bound, so only the median and the fraction of positive values are reported, not the mean.

* alfakR's target is one random bootstrap draw (with noise), the prediction comes from Kriging, s is about 0.5, and R2R is close to 0.
* alfak2's target is the posterior mean of its own full-data fit (already shrunk); after leaving out one frequent karyotype and its neighbours,
  when there are very few frequent karyotypes (≤ 4, mainly λ = 0.2) each held-out fold is low as a whole, and R2R can be very negative.

Against the true fitness, alfak2 is more accurate on most paired inputs (Section 3.1), yet at λ = 0.2 its median CV score is as low as
−2 to −3.5. So the CV score can be used only within one method, to pick minobs or to flag failures, and not to compare two methods.
