## 0. What this round tests

From 8327282 to ff13cf4, alfak2 changed the model in three places:

| commit | change | effect on this benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| af362af | the history of the balance prior is no longer fixed at the sampling interval dt; it is chosen by marginal likelihood among dt and 30, 100, 300, 1000, 3000 divisions (those larger than dt) | point estimates change; fit time about 6–10 times the old one |
| 2d86b39 | the "no cells inside the model domain" error carries the type `alfak2_domain_coverage_error`; each fit reports the fraction of each time point inside the model domain | changes only the error and the diagnostics; successful fits are unchanged |
| ff13cf4 | the uncertainty deficit of unobserved karyotypes changed from 0.6 to 1.1 | changes only the reported intervals; point estimates unchanged |

The alfakR fits are identical to those of round 8327282 (same version, same settings, same inputs); they are reused here directly through symbolic links
(`hpc/link_alfakR.sh`), without refitting. All 3975 alfak2 fits were rerun.

## 1. Conclusions

1. **With two-time-point input, alfak2 is better than alfakR on the great majority of tests, including alfakR using all time points, and this round
   removes the main weakness of the previous round: the case where the two time points are only 1 passage apart.** On ABM data 1 passage apart, the paired comparison's
   median Pearson r at λ = 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.6 is 0.42 / 0.75 / 0.79 (alfakR 0.30 / 0.47 / 0.33;
   8327282 0.30 / 0.60 / 0.69), and alfak2 is more accurate on 84–99% of inputs; the fraction of passage-10 forecasts beating the baseline is
   0.71 (alfakR 0.36, 8327282 0.35); at missegregation rate 8e-4 the median r is 0.50 (alfakR 0.33, 8327282 0.04).
2. **At 3 and 7 passages apart the results are almost the same as 8327282.** Most of these inputs still choose history = dt, and the fits are the same as in the previous round;
   alfak2 is more accurate on 91–100% of inputs (λ ≥ 0.4); forecasts at passage 1 beat the baseline about 0.91 of the time, alfakR two time points about 0.61,
   all time points about 0.74.
3. **On real data (Salehi), alfak2's forecasts win more paired comparisons.** On the same trajectory and the same minobs, the fraction beating the parent
   baseline at the evaluation passages is 0.38–0.51 for alfak2, 0.21–0.23 for alfakR two time points, and 0.24–0.27 for all time points (significant for all four distances).
   The improvement is mainly on trajectories 1 passage apart (same-fit comparison, cosine 0.22 → 0.36, Wasserstein 0.38 → 0.44);
   at 2 or more passages apart it is basically the same as the previous round. The win fractions of both methods are still below 0.5, consistent with the paper.
4. **24 Salehi trajectories are excluded by a data criterion and are no longer counted as alfak2 failures.** Their first and last time points are unrelated at the karyotype level
   (every cell of one time point is more than 2 steps from all major karyotypes of the other time point), which can be determined before fitting, and they are exactly
   the 24 on which alfak2 reports `alfak2_domain_coverage_error` (Section 4.1). alfakR's results on these trajectories
   carry almost no information (median CV score −0.32 to −0.12). After exclusion, the only remaining alfak2 failures are "no frequent karyotypes", and the failure rate
   is not higher than that of alfakR two time points (about half of it at minobs 10 and 20).
5. **Newly found problem: alfak2's optimisation often does not converge.** Among the chosen fits, 10–35% of the nlminb runs did not report convergence
   (similar for ABM and Salehi); the history = dt candidate (that is, the 8327282 fit) itself has 8–24%;
   46–69% of inputs have at least one candidate that did not converge. Choosing among candidates by marginal likelihood assumes that every candidate reached its optimum,
   which is currently not guaranteed (Section 6). This is a problem of alfak2; it does not affect the comparison method of this report, but it is worth
   handling in alfak2.
6. **CV scores still cannot be compared across methods** (Section 7), and **the degradation of alfakR dev2 with all time points at minobs 5** is still present
   (see `docs/report.md` Section 1, item 6; the alfakR results are unchanged).

## 2. Setup

| fit | input |
|---|---|
| alfak2 2 time points | the first and last time points of the window |
| alfakR 2 time points | the same two time points |
| alfakR all time points | all time points of the window (the paper's approach); for the 2-passage window it is the same fit as the row above |
| paper alfakR | the paper's own fits, used only as a reference line |

* ABM: 400 simulations (λ = 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.6, 100 each); the windows are the last 2, 4, 8 passages before day 120,
  with first and last 1, 3, 7 passages apart; minobs 5, 10, 20. Missegregation-rate experiment: 5 missegregation rates × 25 simulations, minobs 10.
* Salehi: 277 trajectories, each of 2 to 10 consecutive passages, with first and last 1 to 9 passages apart; minobs 5, 10, 20.
  Of these, 24 are excluded (Section 4.1), and 253 are analysed.
* Number of fits: ABM 9600 (alfak2 3600, alfakR 6000), missegregation rate 1000, Salehi 2277.

**Two ways of reporting.** "Paper rule": failed fits are dropped; where the paper selects, minobs is picked by each fit's own CV score
(for Salehi, trajectory length is also picked), so different methods are tested on different inputs. "Paired": only the same inputs on which both methods have results are taken,
with no selection.

**Checks.**
* The karyotype sets in the two methods' landscapes (frequent karyotypes, one-step neighbours, neighbours of neighbours) are identical on all 4604
  input × minobs combinations where both succeeded, so the forecasts start from the same set of cells at the last passage.
* "Unanalysable" inputs determined before fitting: 0 among the 1200 ABM inputs and the 375 missegregation-rate inputs; for Salehi, 24 trajectories, exactly the same set as the alfak2
  failures with `alfak2_domain_coverage_error` (all 72 failed fits identified by error type).
* The reproduction check of the scoring code on the paper's fits carries over from the previous round (3261 ABM and 831 Salehi fits identical). This round
  adds some diagnostic columns to the Salehi scoring (Section 4.5); the existing columns are unchanged. The new-karyotype regression now computes only the block of the distance matrix that is used;
  checked locally, its output is identical to the original code, and the number of regressions, 1868, matches the previous round.

**Failure rates.** ABM: alfak2 0/3600; alfakR two time points 79/3600 (2.2%), all time points 61/3600 (1.7%).
All 1000 fits of the missegregation-rate experiment completed. For Salehi see Section 4.1.

**Run cost.** Each alfak2 fit fits up to 6 candidate histories: on ABM the median fit time is 47–76 seconds, the longest
8.3 minutes (8327282: 5–13 seconds, longest 115 seconds); including the forecast, a single task takes at most about 20 minutes, with about 1.2 GB of memory.

## 4. Salehi data

### 4.1 Unanalysable inputs and fit failures

**Exclusion criterion.** Computed from the data alone, before fitting (`R/coverage.R`): from the first and last time points, the model domain is built by the tested alfak2 version's
own rules (the default arguments of `fit_sparse_joint()`, the benchmark's `min_cn = 0`); if any one
time point has 0 cells inside the domain, the trajectory is "unanalysable", and none of its fits (including alfakR all time points and the paper reference line) are used.

* **This criterion is exactly the condition for alfak2 being able to fit**; it excludes exactly the 24 on which alfak2 fails (all in the SA609
  family: 16 at the X4 time point shared by 6 lineages, 4 at SA609TIIDT X4, 2 each at SA609UnBU X4 and X7). They are still excluded for
  all methods, because the first and last time points of these inputs are unrelated at the karyotype level, alfakR's results on them carry almost no information
  (table below), and there are signs of data-quality problems (in the time points concerned, the median distance between cells within the same time point is 2.5–8 steps; at other time points, 0–1 steps).
* **"No shared karyotype between first and last" is not used as the criterion**: 81 of the 277 have no shared karyotype, and alfak2 fits 57 of these normally.

### 4.5 Sensitivity analysis: fraction of the input inside the alfak2 model domain (`figures/coverage_salehi.png`)

**Coverage**: the lower "fraction of cells inside the domain" of the first and last time points. Used only for grouping, not as an exclusion criterion.

| coverage | (0, 0.1) | [0.1, 0.2) | [0.2, 0.5) | [0.5, 1] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| trajectories | 25 | 34 | 67 | 127 |
| median interval (passages) | 5 | 3 | 2 | 2 |

Coverage falls with the interval (median 0.68 at 1 passage apart, 0.24 at 5 passages, 0.08 at 9 passages; Spearman −0.29).

**Paired comparison grouped by coverage** (Wasserstein, vs alfakR two time points): (0, 0.1) 0.80 vs 0.17 (23 fits,
p = 2e-4); [0.1, 0.2) 0.59 vs 0.17 (23, p = 0.006); [0.2, 0.5) 0.60 vs 0.31 (78, p = 3e-6);
[0.5, 1] 0.45 vs 0.19 (247, p = 2e-12). The lower the coverage, the larger alfak2's advantage, but it is still significant at high coverage;
among inputs with coverage ≥ 0.5, split further by interval, alfak2 is higher at 1 / 2 / 3–4 / 5+ passages apart (0.41 vs 0.13, 0.46 vs 0.28,
0.41 vs 0.19, 0.71 vs 0.14).

**Confounding with the interval.** The paired difference (win fraction of alfak2 minus that of alfakR two time points) regressed on log10(coverage) and the interval,
with a cluster bootstrap over the 12 source lines (SA609 etc.): the slope of coverage is negative, but its interval includes 0 (Wasserstein −0.25 [−0.69, 0.10]);
at coverage 1 alfak2 still leads (Wasserstein: 1 passage apart +0.16 [0.04, 0.25], 3 passages +0.24 [0.04, 0.44];
Euclidean: 1 passage apart +0.25 [0.08, 0.41]). So a substantial part of the trend that the advantage grows as coverage falls can be explained by
the interval; after accounting for the interval, the effect of coverage itself is uncertain.

**Test of the "denoising" explanation.** Hypothesis: for low-coverage inputs the parent is very dispersed, and if alfak2 merely pulled the forecast onto the major karyotypes,
it could also get closer to the daughter. For this, a baseline that uses no model was added: restrict the parent to its high-frequency karyotypes (fraction ≥ 1% and
≥ 3 cells) and renormalise (Wasserstein, alfak2's own fits × daughters):

| coverage | alfak2 beats parent | high-frequency-only parent beats parent | alfak2 beats high-frequency-only parent | entropy change: alfak2 forecast / daughter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (0, 0.1) | 0.85 | 0.29 | 0.76 | −0.02 / −0.30 |
| [0.1, 0.2) | 0.68 | 0.31 | 0.80 | −0.02 / −0.34 |
| [0.2, 0.5) | 0.62 | 0.52 | 0.74 | −0.06 / −0.16 |
| [0.5, 1] | 0.45 | 0.42 | 0.49 | −0.12 / +0.08 |

* **At low coverage, the "denoising" explanation does not hold**: the baseline keeping only high-frequency karyotypes rarely beats the parent, and the entropy of alfak2's forecast hardly
  falls (the daughter's entropy falls by 0.3); alfak2's wins come from elsewhere.
* **At high coverage, alfak2 is on par with this simple baseline**: win fraction 0.45 vs 0.42, and alfak2 beats it in only 49% of cases;
  on the overlap distance the simple baseline is even better (0.48 vs 0.39). alfakR two time points at the same coverage reaches only 0.19,
  worse than this baseline (0.43). That is, on high-coverage real data, a substantial part of alfak2's advantage over alfakR
  can be achieved by "not spreading probability over rare karyotypes".

## 5. Stability: change with the interval between the two time points

* **ABM** (`figures/stability_by_window.png`): the accuracy of both methods rises with the interval. In 8327282, alfak2
  gained most from 1 to 3 passages (at λ = 0.8, median R² 0.08 → 0.65); in this round 1 passage already gives 0.52, and the gain from the interval
  is mainly only the 0.52 → 0.65 from 1 → 3 passages. alfakR two time points 0.04 → 0.33. Between 3 and 7 passages both are basically stable.
* **Missegregation rate**: at 1 passage apart, alfak2 is above alfakR at every missegregation rate (8327282 was below alfakR at 4e-4 and 8e-4).
* **Salehi**: alfak2 is above alfakR two time points at 1 to 6 passages apart and does not get worse with the interval; the win fraction of alfakR two time points
  falls to 0.16 or below at 4 passages and more.
* The previous round's finding that "short intervals are alfak2's weak point" basically no longer holds in this round.

  the output under the default settings), but it makes the history choice noisy, and it may be where alfak2 still has room to improve.
  Recommended in alfak2: raise `iter.max` / `eval.max` or retry from another starting point, and warn when not converged.

## 7. Why CV scores cannot be compared across methods

The CV score of both packages is R2R = 1 − Σ(p′ − o′)² / Σo′² (p, o centred), which equals 2rs − s²,
where r is the correlation and s the ratio of the standard deviations of prediction and target; it has no lower bound, so only the median and the fraction positive are reported, not the mean.

* alfakR's target is one random bootstrap draw (with noise), and the prediction comes from Kriging; s is about 0.4–0.6, and R2R is close to 0.
* alfak2's target is the posterior mean of its own full-data fit (already shrunk); after one frequent karyotype and its neighbours are held out,
  when there are very few frequent karyotypes (≤ 4, mainly λ = 0.2), each held-out fold is low as a whole, and R2R can be very negative.

In this round, at λ = 0.2 alfak2's median CV score is still −3.5 to −2.1, while against the true fitness it is more accurate than alfakR on 91–96% of
paired inputs. So the CV score can only be used within one method to pick minobs or flag failure, not to
compare the two methods.

## 9. Caveats and follow-up

1. alfak2's optimisation convergence problem (Section 6) should be handled in alfak2; after that the history choice may change, and then
   the results of this round at 1 and 3 passages apart need to be retested.
2. On high-coverage real data, alfak2's forecasts are on par with the simple baseline "keep only the parent's high-frequency karyotypes" (Section 4.5).
   When alfak2's forecast advantage on real data is reported in future, this baseline should be reported alongside it.
3. alfak2's advantage in Fig. 3 still needs to be retested with the "distance to the nearest major karyotype" term removed (Section 4.4).
4. The degradation of alfakR dev2 with all time points at minobs 5 (`docs/report.md` Section 1, item 6) may affect PANcanKFLs.
5. Run details of this round: the alfakR fits are linked from `results/`; 12 alfak2 tasks were interrupted by node failures and rerun
   (no results had been written before the interruption); the new-karyotype regression in the collection script used to exceed memory with many cores and lose results; it was changed to compute only
   the distance blocks that are used, and to stop with an error when results are lost.
