## 0. What this round tests

This is the first time alfak2 uses all time points of a trajectory, not only the first and last ones.

| commit | Change | Effect on this benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| 21aedca | `fit_sparse_joint()` can fit more than two time points; the extra settings all have defaults:<br>• major karyotypes are taken only from the first and last time points (`intermediate = "majors"`), and the model domain is built around the major karyotypes<br>• the weight of intermediate time points is 1/max(1, dispersion of the major karyotypes)<br>• a new convergence check: `diagnostics$convergence` 0 means accepted, 1 means not accepted | alfak2 has an "all time points" fit for the first time; convergence rates cannot be compared with earlier rounds |
| 8512694 | An intermediate time point with no cells on the major karyotypes is dropped from the fit (`diagnostics$dropped_samples`, warning class `alfak2_samples_left_out_warning`); a guard for when the inner Hessian is not finite | The all-time-points fit only requires the first and last time points to have cells inside the model domain, the same exclusion rule as ends |

- The alfakR fits are exactly those of round 8327282, reused through symbolic links (`hpc/link_alfakR.sh results`).
- All 3107 alfak2 tasks were rerun:
  - first and last time points (ends): 1852;
  - all time points (all): 1255.
- Each task fits once and exports three sets of output, minobs 5, 10, 20: 8071 minobs fits in total.

## 1. Conclusions

1. **On the same time points, the alfak2 all-time-points fit is better than the alfakR all-time-points fit on every test.**
   - Paired median Pearson r of the ABM landscapes:
     - 4-passage windows: 0.66 vs 0.40, alfak2 higher on 92% of inputs;
     - 8-passage windows: 0.71 vs 0.45, 89%.
   - Fraction of passage-10 forecasts that beat the "no evolution" baseline (Wasserstein): 0.87 vs 0.45, 0.92 vs 0.49.
   - Missegregation-rate experiment: the gap is smallest for 8-passage windows (median r 0.82 vs 0.80, alfak2 higher in 66%, p = 0.004).
   - Salehi (trajectories of 3–10 passages):
     - fraction of forecasts that beat the parent baseline: Wasserstein 0.54 vs 0.27, Euclidean 0.42 vs 0.26;
     - fitness of new karyotypes predicts their emergence: 0.52 vs 0.24.
2. **The two-time-point results are the same as ff13cf4** (Section 6). The two-time-point comparison of alfak2 vs alfakR is therefore unchanged,
   see `docs/report_ff13cf4.md`.
3. **Within alfak2, adding the intermediate time points did not make the landscape more accurate; the forecast is slightly better.**
   - On ABM, compared with using only the first and last time points, the all-time-points landscape is slightly worse at λ ≤ 0.8:
     - λ = 0.4, 8-passage windows: r 0.54 vs 0.60, all time points higher in only 11%;
     - better only at λ = 1.6: 0.89 vs 0.87, 81%.
   - The forecast is better on 8-passage windows: it beats the baseline at passage 10 in 0.91 vs 0.86 (Euclidean, McNemar p = 8e-11).
   - On Salehi the two have similar forecast win rates (Wasserstein 0.52 vs 0.52), and the predicted direction is more accurate: median angle 70° vs 80°.
   - This point must be handed over to the multi-timepoint side: the current anchored design mainly improved the forecast, not the landscape.
4. **The Salehi exclusions are the same as for ends.**
   - The all-time-points design excludes exactly the 24 trajectories that ends excludes; no method uses them.
   - 21aedca would exclude 61.
   - Of the 187 analysable multi-passage trajectories, 53 had intermediate time points dropped by alfak2, 103 time points in total;
     10 of them had all intermediate time points dropped, which amounts to falling back to a two-time-point fit.
   - These are mainly clonal-sweep trajectories. On this subset alfak2 is still clearly better than alfakR:
     forecast beats the parent 0.64 vs 0.11 (Wasserstein, 48 pairs), new karyotypes 0.47 vs 0.11 (92 pairs).
5. **Cost: alfak2 is an order of magnitude faster and uses 5–10 times less memory.**
   - Median time of the all-time-points fit: ABM 5.1 vs 59 minutes, missegregation rate 5.3 vs 74 minutes, Salehi 3.6 vs 26 minutes.
   - Median peak memory per input: ABM 1.8 vs 12.6 GB, missegregation rate 2.6 vs 25.7 GB, Salehi 1.6 vs 8.0 GB.
   - Maximum: alfak2 7.1 GB, alfakR 147 GB.
6. **The new convergence check does not accept 10–41% of fits** (Section 7).
   - Highest for ABM 8-passage windows: all time points 41%, first and last time points 36%.
   - This is the new criterion of 21aedca (`diagnostics$convergence` = 1), not the nlminb return code, and cannot be compared with the ff13cf4 rates.
   - The comparisons in this report are not affected, but it is an item alfak2 needs to look at.
7. **For CV scores only the median and the fraction above 0 are reported**; they cannot be compared across methods (reasons in `docs/report.md`).

## 2. Settings

| Fit | Input |
|---|---|
| alfak2 2 time points | the first and last time points of the window or trajectory |
| alfak2 all time points | all time points (new); the default setting of 8512694 ("anchored") |
| alfakR 2 time points | the first and last time points |
| alfakR all time points | all time points (what the paper does) |
| paper alfakR | the paper's own fits, as a reference only |

- **ABM**: 400 simulations (λ = 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.6). The windows are the last 2, 4, 8 passages before day 120,
  with the first and last 1, 3, 7 passages apart. The all-time-points design only has the 4- and 8-passage windows (w4_all, w8_all);
  the 2-passage window has only two time points.
- **Missegregation-rate experiment**: 5 missegregation rates × 25 simulations, minobs 10.
- **Salehi**: 277 trajectories, each of 2–10 consecutive passages. 72 have only 2 passages and no separate all-time-points fit;
  the other 205 do.
- **Groups in the "summary figure"**:
  - "2 time points": the first and last time points;
  - "all time points": only inputs with more than two time points;
  - accuracy is compared only on the same inputs where both methods have results (paired); failures, time and memory are counted over all inputs.

## 5. Excluded inputs and dropped intermediate time points

- **Exclusion rule** (`R/coverage.R`): decided before fitting, from the data only, with the tested alfak2's own functions
  (`sparse_joint_major_columns()`, `sparse_joint_domain()`, `sparse_joint_uncounted_samples()`).
  Both the first and the last time point must have cells inside the model domain. Each design (ends, all) is judged on the time points it fits itself, and the rule is shared by all methods.
- **Agreement with alfak2's own check**:
  - Salehi: 205/205 agree; ABM: 800/800; missegregation rate: 250/250.
  - The predicted number of intermediate time points to be dropped agrees with the number the fit actually dropped, on all analysable inputs.
  - The probe used: first fit with `intermediate_weight = 2`, which is rejected only after the coverage check.
- **ABM and missegregation rate**: no exclusions, and no intermediate time points dropped.
- **Intermediate time-point weights**:
  - ABM and missegregation rate: median 1, minimum 0.66;
  - Salehi: median 0.71, minimum 0.07; the dispersion of real data is much higher.
- **Salehi exclusions**:
  - ends and all are the same 24 (`analysis/salehi/exclusion_check.tsv`), exactly the same set as those for which alfak2 reports
    `alfak2_domain_coverage_error`.
  - The all-time-points design counts only trajectories with more than 2 passages, which gives 18; the other 6 have only 2 passages.
- **Salehi dropped intermediate time points**: of the 187 analysable multi-passage trajectories, 53 had 103 time points dropped, and 10 of them had all intermediate time points dropped. By family:

| Family | SA000 | SA001 | SA535 | SA609 | SA609H1 | SA609H2 | SA609R2 | SA609R2b | SA609TIIDT | SA609UnBU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trajectories | 9 | 9 | 2 | 8 | 6 | 3 | 9 | 3 | 3 | 1 |

  These are mostly clonal sweeps: the intermediate time points are two or more steps away from the major karyotypes of the first and last time points.

## 6. Two-time-point fits are the same as ff13cf4 (`analysis/compare_rounds/results_ff13cf4_summary.tsv`)

The two-time-point code path of 8512694 is the same as in ff13cf4, so the fits on the first and last time points should be unchanged. `scripts/compare_rounds.R` checks fit by fit:

- Statuses identical: Salehi 831, ABM 3600, missegregation rate 375.
- Landscapes identical within numerical rounding: maximum difference of the means 2.2e-16, of the standard deviations 6.7e-16; but not a single one is bitwise identical.
- Cross-validation output and history identical.
- Forecast replicates all bitwise identical: 3996.
- Score differences: median 1e-16, 99% quantile 4e-3, maximum 0.038 (all at λ = 0.2). This is the landscape difference of 1e-16 changing
  the order of tied values in the Spearman correlation, not different fits.
